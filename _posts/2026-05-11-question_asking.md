## Purpose
Seeking information through asking questions starts in early childhood, with children learning to generate better quality question as they get older. Learning can be enhanced by educational strategies the encourage asking questions and teach children to ask better questions, with some evidence suggesting that question asking is a more efficient predictor of memory than curiosity. However, the difference between the impact of self-generated and non-self-generated questions on curiosity-driven learning is still under investigated.  Here, we aim to determine if curiosity elicited differs between self-generated and non-self-generated questions and how this affects subsequent learning in young adolescents.


## Method
We are recruiting children ages 12-14 to take part in an adapted 3-part trivia paradigm. In the first phase, participants are presented with short sorties and either given related pre-written questions, or asked to write their own related question. They are then asked to rate each question for how curious they are to find out the answer. All participants see 50 pre-written questions and are asked to try and write 50 of their own questions. Up to 40 self-generated and 40 pre-generated questions are then selected to progress to the encoding phase of the experiment, where participants are once again presented with the questions in a randomised orders and asked to rate their curiosity again before receiving the answer to the question and rating how interesting they found the answer. Finally, during the retrieval phase, participants undergo a memory test for the answers that were presented to them in the encoding phase. We will look at differences in curiosity, interest and memory between the self-generated and non-self generated questions.
